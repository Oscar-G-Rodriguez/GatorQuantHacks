# Cross-variable discovery for the Massive track

This follow-up asks which disclosure labels describe different market responses, and whether their relationship changes with other observed variables. Oscar authorized it after reviewing the first development results, then limited additional sponsor data to what helps the Massive track. The first CFO/CEO volatility differences are leads selected from the v3 exploration. Reusing 2024–2025 to investigate them supplies further discovery, not independent confirmation.

The challenge's core is Massive disclosure labels, historical option reference data and option prices for the supplied 100-company universe. Its eight fixed horizons are 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 21, 42 and 63 sessions, followed by expiry where observable. The final notebook must reproduce from a Massive API key. These requirements were freshly read on the [organizer's Massive page](https://www.gqhacks.com/tracks/systematic-trading/massive) on October 3 through the browser; the web reader could not render that page. External-feed eligibility is not established by being an event sponsor. Keep optional Databento research evidence separate from the Massive-only replay until that question is resolved.

## The bounded combinations

The calculations below distinguish relationships between inputs, adjusted event responses, and later-outcome prediction. A relationship can be useful for understanding the data even if no future forecasting benefit is expected.

| Combination | Useful calculation | Outcome or interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CFO/CEO label × observation-time past volatility | Fixed-issuer interaction regression, including both main effects and five past controls | Does the later return/RMS-volatility association vary with the volatility known at the observation close? |
| CFO/CEO label × observation-time past 20-session return | Same adjusted interaction and chronological feature addition | Does the response vary with recent price direction? |
| CFO/CEO label × earnings, guidance or compensation on the same filing | Exact CIK/accession co-tag counts, adjusted interaction if both groups have support | Helps separate a leadership label from accompanying news. Same date alone is insufficient. |
| Disclosure-context variables × each other and past controls | Early-development pairwise Pearson/Spearman with usable row/issuer counts | Describes overlap and helps avoid counting several proxies as independent information. |
| Disclosure type × option-implied move | Compare later absolute movement and option-premium change within a fixed maturity/mark cohort | Future options stage. A straddle-premium proxy describes movement through expiry; it is not isolated event uncertainty. |
| Disclosure type × option maturity and achieved moneyness | Fixed 90–180-day baseline, declared shorter-maturity sensitivity, comparable call/put cohorts | Future options stage. Different contracts must not masquerade as the same exposure. |
| Disclosure type × spread, quote age and volume | Response comparisons and retained exclusion counts across prespecified quality groups | Future options stage. Helps distinguish response differences from poor or asynchronous marks. |
| Disclosure type × dated open interest or put/call shape | Backward availability joins, supported subgroup descriptions and later ablation | Optional enrichment. No same-day OI assumption, current-snapshot IV history or unvalidated American-option skew claim. |

The current stock diagnostics use the assumed observation close after filing+1 calendar day. Their rolling past controls can already contain the filing reaction. They are not clean pre-filing states. Historical vendor classification delivery remains unverified, so even successful later prediction cannot establish an executable disclosure signal. The options stage must retain a separate pre-filing diagnostic clock and later label-available clock, synchronized backward marks, quote ages, contract identities, multipliers/deliverables, missing reasons and hard development end. No option outcome or expiry may extend into 2026 for this discovery.

## What is implemented for the follow-up

[discovery-massive-followup.json](../../config/discovery-massive-followup.json) preserves the original universe and two lead categories, adds the eight fixed session horizons, and sets 63-session date blocks. [prepare_disclosure_context.py](../../tools/prepare_disclosure_context.py) downloads context on the PC; [discovery/context.py](../../discovery/context.py) attaches it to a new immutable snapshot. Unknown issuer context remains missing, early events are not reassigned to the first usable day, and exact co-tags are restricted to original lead filing identities. The original v3 snapshot and results remain unchanged.

H06 now retains matched-pair identities and differences, standardized covariate balance, the median contrast and leave-one-issuer mean range. Its matching rule is unchanged. These diagnostics expose poor comparability and concentration; paired dependent confidence intervals remain unimplemented. H07 keeps the unsupported ordered-sequence comparisons and adds ten bounded combinations: each of two categories with two continuous past controls and three co-tag groups, across both targets and eight horizons. Continuous scales fit in the earliest 40% of dates; regression measurements use the remaining development dates and fixed issuer effects. Co-tag interactions need at least eight events in both with/without groups, and every unsupported design remains inconclusive. Coefficients are descriptive without intervals.

H08 adds a Ridge family with disclosure × past-control terms scaled inside each chronological training fold, and paired errors on labeled event validation rows in addition to the pooled cohort. Purging still uses actual target-end dates. The existing tree's 30-row minimum leaf size is checked before fitting: a candidate unable to form two supported training leaves is inconclusive, rather than evidence of no nonlinear effect. No new tuning search or smaller-leaf rescue was introduced. H10 adds training-only pairwise context relationships with coverage counts. H11 includes every new planned prediction/cohort family, including unsupported variants. Sparse event-date cohorts and fewer than four 63-session blocks cannot receive a bootstrap interval; 999 draws also generally cannot resolve the expanded search-adjusted tails. More compute does not remove those limits.

Twenty-nine small offline checks passed locally in 1.013 seconds before transfer. New checks verify exact filing co-tags versus unrelated same-day filings, missing issuer context, early-event alignment, rare-candidate tree support and recovery of a known synthetic interaction with issuer effects. Run-specific outputs and subsequent interpretation are recorded there and in the hypothesis logs; a proposed or queued run is never a completed result.
